## The eight interview answers

1. **Vibe.** Mission-first. Disciplined. Zero-defect. Quiet confidence. References: a mission-planning board in a dark ops room (one light source, paper on the table); the low-key grade of *Zero Dark Thirty*; a field manual set in condensed caps.
2. **The scroll journey, in order.** First the two states of knowledge side by side: the operator in the dark, the plain sheet of paper. Then what scattered knowledge actually costs a program, in the Government's own words. Then the turn: the loose files cross over and file themselves into one governed environment. Then the six task areas as a ledger. Then the weight of the record: customers' words against the real numbers. Then the reach: four networks against thirteen commands. Last: the point of contact, one action, the small print.
3. **Energy curve.** Calm open. Rising unease. The peak is the turn, and it is not loud: it is the most *ordered* moment on the page. Dense, steady middle. One lift on the numbers. Quiet, resolved close.
4. **Feeling, stage by stage, and the one moment.** Recognition, unease, clarity, rigor, weight, reach, resolve. The moment: *the pile of loose files on the dark side slides through the divider and shelves itself, one by one, under my hand; then the divider gives way and the shelf takes the whole screen.*
5. **One thing no site they have seen does.** The page is a divided sheet, and the argument literally crosses the divide: fragments of the problem physically migrate across the seam into the solution as you scroll, and the seam itself moves.
6. **Aesthetic range.** Dense-editorial with premium-minimal restraint. Two grounds (night and paper), one red, the brand's own condensed caps. Not brutalist, not maximalist.
7. **One world or scenes?** Neither: one *stage*, two grounds, held for the whole page. Hard edge between them, never a crossfade. It is a split, not a world and not chapters.
8. **Assets owned.** Hero photograph (SOF operators, 2880×1920), white shield logo, twelve agency seals (grayscale), Exavier Watson portrait, event flyer (2550×3300). No footage, no generation key, no spend. The hero clip is rendered from the owned still (a slow push-in). Nothing else is generated.

## Grammar: Split stage (uniqueness.md §2.7)

Two columns held in tension for the whole page, resolved by scroll. Left ground: night (`#0c0f14`), the scattered state. Right ground: paper (`#fafaf9`), the governed state. Hard edge, no drift. The **divider is the chrome**: it carries the two labels (SCATTERED / GOVERNED), the shield mark, and the page's progress as a fill along its length. No bar. The hero establishes the split at 50/50 with both headlines readable at once. The close is the **collapse**: the divider travels to the left edge, the paper column takes the full width, and the CTA lives in the winning column.

Why the other seven lost:

- **Filmic one-shot**: one owned still, no footage, no key. One clip cannot carry a film, and the brief's argument is a comparison, not a carried ride.
- **Chaptered editorial**: the current page already reads as a document with headings; a folio version would be the same page with page numbers. It also forbids the media-column hero the one strong photograph deserves.
- **Live surface**: we do not have the real EKM surface, and a div-built SharePoint or Power BI is exactly the fake dashboard taste.md bans. A defense contractor cannot ship a painted product.
- **Continuous world**: requires generated flight legs. No key, no spend, and the brief is not about travel through a place.
- **Typographic poster**: the brand has a strong sentence but also a photograph, thirteen commands and a record of numbers; type alone throws away half the evidence.
- **Gallery / catalog**: nothing here is a range of objects to walk past.
- **Rhythmic cutlist**: the wrong pulse for a zero-defect services brand; nothing about USSOCOM knowledge management wants a cut per second.

## Signature move: the filing

Eight fragments, each a real place program knowledge was found to live (from the case studies: separate portals, project tools, ticketing systems, databases, spreadsheets, email threads, local files, disconnected tools), lie scattered on the dark side of the peak act. As the act's `--sc-p` advances, each fragment (staggered, overlapping) translates across the seam, un-rotates, and lands in its own empty row on the paper side. The row marks itself filled. When the last one lands, the seam itself tips from 50% to 46%, the first movement of the divider on the page, and a single held line arrives: *Zero data loss. Through Top Secret.* Positions are measured from the DOM, so the same code files top-to-bottom on a phone where the stage is stacked. Coded in the page, reading `--sc-p`; the engine is untouched. Under reduced motion the fragments are already filed.
